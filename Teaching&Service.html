Teaching Experience

Courses as Instructor of Record at Wayne State University

These are courses for which I designed the syllabus, created all course materials, including lectures, assignments, and tests, delivered all lectures, and completed all grading independently. I enjoy teaching, and I greatly value the opportunity to impact my students' education and lives. My student feedback reflects success in this endeavor; my students enjoy my energetic, personable, and caring approach to teaching.

Introduction to the Anthropology of Business (ANT 2050)

  • Taught independently 10x from Fall 2022 through Spring/Summer 2026. Seven of these appointments were in person, and three were online.
  • This class meets Wayne State's General Education Global Learning requirement.
  • Wayne State University is the birthplace of Business and Organizational Anthropology. The subfield of cultural anthropology was created by Dr. Marietta Baba during her time at Wayne State, and it has grown in international popularity in the decades since.
  • In my version of the course, I center the usefulness of qualitative methods and an anthropological perspective in fields that usually use quantitative methods and profit-centric models almost exclusively. The structure of the course is survey style, covering modules on white collar and corporate work, business and labor organization, retail, products, and goods, the entertainment industry, and nonprofits and causes. I hold students' attention by featuring topics selected to pique a broad range of students' interests, including lectures on Netflix, anime, and more!

Introduction to Anthropology (ANT 1100)

  • Taught independently twice; once in Winter 2025 and the other in Fall 2025. Both were in-person sections.
  • This class meets Wayne State's General Education Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion (DEI) and Social Inquiry (SI) requirements.
  • This is a standard four-field introduction to anthropology course covering biological anthropology, archaeology, cultural anthropology, and linguistic anthropology.

Introduction to Biological Anthropology (ANT 2110)

  • Taught independently online in the Spring/Summer 2024 semester.
  • This class meets Wayne State's General Education Natural Science Inquiry (NSI) requirement.
  • Modules in my syllabus include genetics and evolution, modern humans, primates, fossils and skeletons, and early humans.
